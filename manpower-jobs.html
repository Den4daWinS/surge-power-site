The Work

What Trade Labor & Helper Workers Do at Surge Power

Our trade labor placements span commercial construction, industrial facilities, and trade support roles. Here's what the day-to-day typically looks like across our most common labor positions:

Material Handling & Staging

Receiving, organizing, and moving materials across active job sites, keeping crews supplied and work areas clear so trades can stay productive.

Demolition & Site Prep

Performing controlled demo work, stripping out old finishes, clearing debris, and prepping surfaces ahead of trade crews on commercial renovation and new construction projects.

Trade Helper Support

Assisting licensed electricians, HVAC technicians, and plumbers, pulling wire, handling tools, running materials, and supporting rough-in and finish work on commercial projects.

Industrial Production Support

Supporting operations at manufacturing and industrial facilities: equipment staging, production line assistance, maintenance support, and general plant labor.

Site Cleanup & Safety

Maintaining clean, hazard-free job sites by managing waste removal, organizing tools, and enforcing PPE and safety protocols throughout the work day.

Crew & Foreman Support

Working directly under foremen and lead tradespeople, following instructions, coordinating with other trades, and contributing to project milestones on schedule.

What We Look For

Requirements for Trade Labor Placement

We staff both entry-level and experienced positions. You don't need a resume that goes back 20 years, you need a strong work ethic, reliability, and the right attitude for commercial and industrial jobsites.

  • Reliable transportation to job sites across the region
  • Ability to work a consistent schedule: commercial jobs don't flex around no-shows
  • Basic knowledge of jobsite safety and PPE requirements
  • OSHA 10 card preferred (not always required for entry-level labor positions)
  • Ability to pass a background check and drug screening
  • Valid government-issued ID and eligibility to work in the U.S.
  • Trade certifications or licenses a plus (electrical, HVAC, plumbing)
  • Prior commercial or industrial field experience preferred for trade labor roles
